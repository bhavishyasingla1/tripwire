| Tripwire category | What the sources say (paraphrased) | Listed by |
|---|---|---|
| Asks for a fee or deposit | Real employers don't charge to hire. Watch for fees for kits, training or "job guarantees", and for upfront placement fees. Task scams require deposits to "unlock" earnings, then freeze them. Fees are requested at the application stage. In LinkedIn's survey, 56% cited upfront payments as a red flag. | FTC-J, FTC-T, PIB-23, LI-H, LI-P, LI-F |
| Moves chat to Telegram or WhatsApp | An agent starts the chat on WhatsApp or Telegram after an ad. The scam opens with an unexpected text or WhatsApp message. Recruiters push urgently to move to WhatsApp, Telegram or outside forms. 90% of reported scam messages move the chat off-platform. Don't send money to an unknown person contacting you there. | PIB-23, FTC-T, LI-H, LI-P (L2), LI-F |
| Free-mail or look-alike domain | Recruiter uses Gmail or Yahoo instead of a company domain. Domains don't match, or contact details can't be verified. Fake email addresses and letterheads imitate RBI, which says recruitment news appears only on rbi.org.in. A fake "mission" website copied a ministry and called for applications. Check that the role is on the company's own careers site. | LI-H, RBI-18, PIB-25, LI-P |
| Pay far above market, or pay for trivial tasks | Ads promise big money for little work. Tasks are liking videos, rating products or rating map listings, with small early payouts and "very high commission" schemes. Offers seem too good to be true. Postings stress first-year pay or a signing bonus. | FTC-J, FTC-T, PIB-23, LI-F, LI-H |
| Urgency or pressure | Pressure to act fast (45% in LinkedIn's survey). "Instant selection" and tight deadlines. Pressure to move off-platform right away. Task scams add victims to group chats of fake success stories when they hesitate. | LI-P, I4C-26, LI-H, LI-F, FTC-T |
| Asks for ID (Aadhaar/PAN) or bank details early | Requests for ID photos, banking details or national ID numbers before formal hiring. Being asked for sensitive information early (59% in LinkedIn's survey). Aadhaar, banking or biometric data requested without a verified purpose. PIB-23 also says to check the receiver's name in the UPI app, because a random person's name suggests a mule account. | LI-H, LI-P, I4C-26, PIB-23 |
| No interview | "Instant selection", or fake online interviews and "verification" steps used to collect data. **Partial:** LinkedIn's "hiring wraps up unusually fast" guidance was seen only in a search snippet and is UNVERIFIED. | I4C-26 |
| Vague role | Unexpected offer of "online work" with no specifics. Vague job details. Anonymous posters who can't be verified. Unverifiable contacts or dead websites. | FTC-T, LI-P, LI-H, FTC-J |

## 3. Reporting steps (URLs checked, HTTP 200 on 23 Nov 2026)
**India**
- **National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal**, https://cybercrime.gov.in, run by I4C/MHA. Use it to report any cybercrime, including phone numbers and social handles used by fraudsters (PIB-23, I1).
- **Helpline 1930**: toll-free help with filing a cyber-fraud complaint. If money was lost, call quickly so the funds can be frozen through CFCFRMS (I1).
- **Sanchar Saathi "Chakshu"** (DoT), https://sancharsaathi.gov.in/sfc/. The page is titled "Report Suspected Fraud Communication / UCC (Spam)". It is for calls, SMS and WhatsApp messages where **no money has been lost yet**. Report within **30 days**. "Online job / lottery / gifts / loan offers" is one of its categories. Cases with actual financial loss go to I4C (cybercrime.gov.in / 1930), per I2.
- **LinkedIn**: report a posting through the three-dot menu, or a profile through More → Report/Block (LI-P, LI-F).

**United States**
- **FTC**: https://reportfraud.ftc.gov. LinkedIn Help also points job-scam victims here (LI-H).
- **FBI IC3**: https://www.ic3.gov.
- If you paid a scammer, contact the payment company at once and ask for a reversal (FTC-J).

## 4. Real cases (inspiration only; any sample messages built from them must be clearly labelled as fictional)
1. **Bengaluru, Telegram "Amazon India" task scam.** A 47-year-old joined a Telegram group posing as "Amazon India Part-Time benefit job" on 11 Jan 2025. He was given product and video review tasks, then "paid tasks" he had to fund himself, and lost **₹67.63 lakh**. Deccan Herald, 8 Oct 2025. https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/bengaluru/moonlighting-gone-awry-bengaluru-man-loses-over-rs-67-lakh-in-part-time-job-fraud-3756646
2. **Delhi, website-review job that turned into "prepaid" Bitcoin tasks.** Contact came over Telegram with ₹50 per review. The victim was then pushed into deposits to "redeem" his earnings and lost **₹17.49 lakh**. Police arrested 4 people. Lokmat Times (IANS), 22 Jul 2025. https://www.lokmattimes.com/national/work-from-home-job-fraud-racket-busted-in-delhi-four-arrested
3. **Delhi, fake "Bretish Airways" airport jobs.** Instagram ads and fake HR tele-callers used data bought from job portals. Candidates paid ₹7,000–10,000 "registration" fees and received fake offer letters and an NOC. Police arrested 6 people. Telangana Today, 24 Aug 2026. https://telanganatoday.com/six-arrested-for-fake-airline-job-racket-using-bretish-airways-websites
4. **Official example: a fake government recruitment site.** A fake "National Rural Development and Recreation Mission" website copied the Rural Development Ministry and invited job applications. I4C took the sites down. PIB, 4 Apr 2025. https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2118851
